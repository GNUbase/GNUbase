# MIDI effects

The MIDI insert and send slots of MIDI and instrument tracks, and GNUbase's built-in MIDI effects that play in them (#135, reference manual pp. 1104–1106). They change the track's events as it plays them and add new ones; the notes in the document stay as they are. The slots are the channel's `midiInserts` and `midiSends` in `channel.set` and `project.get`, so also the MCP tool `channel_set` and `gnubase.channel.set` in Lua, and they are saved in GNUbase's entry of the project file. `midiEffect.list` lists the effects with their parameters.

- **The slots** (`midi::EffectRack` in `src/engine/midi/effects.h`): each section has four slots and `bypass`, which bypasses all of them; the inserts also have the `divider` (0 to 4, the slots above it), the sends `pre`. A slot has an `effect` (an id from `midiEffect.list`, or empty for none), `on` and the effect's `parameters`, by key (`mode`, `rate`, ...), each a number or the name of one of its options (`"Up/Down"`, `"1/16"`); `project.get` shows numbers. A send's slot also has an `output`: the channel of an instrument track its effect plays to, or 0 for the track's own output (an instrument track's own instrument, or the one a MIDI track is routed to, which a send to it is too). Two tracks can't send to each other this way, nor round a longer loop, and removing a track clears the outputs of the sends that played to it. `channel.set` changes only what it is given, also within each slot (`slots: [{}, {"on": false}]` switches the second one off); a slot that gets another effect starts from that effect's defaults.
- **How they play:** the track's own events, those of its parts (after the MIDI modifiers, [midi-modifiers.md](midi-modifiers.md)) and those played into it (`midi.inject`, MIDI thru), go through the inserts in order, each effect playing what the one before it played. Each send plays its effect on the same events, after the inserts or, with `pre`, before the MIDI modifiers and the inserts (p. 1106), and what it plays goes to the track's output beside what the inserts play: the unprocessed events and the effect's together, as on p. 1106. A send to the track's own output gets the notes only (note-ons, note-offs and poly pressure), so the track's controllers, pitch bend, program changes and SysEx reach the output once, through the inserts; a send to another instrument track gets every event, since that instrument needs the controllers, pitch bend and sustain too.
- **Before the modifiers** (`pre`): the render snapshot holds the parts' notes with the track's modifiers applied, so while `pre` sends play and the modifiers change notes, it also holds them without the modifiers (`RenderChannel::preNotes`, with the parts' own transpose and velocity, the drum and articulation maps and the chord track), and a second sequencer, `MidiSequence::pre`, plays those and the input as it came in, for the sends. With modifiers that change nothing, the sends take the events the inserts take.
- **Sends to another instrument track** (p. 1106): the graph has a node per such send (`graph::NodeKind::MidiSend`), after the node the track's sequencer runs in, which sends what the send's effect played in the block (`MidiSequencer::sendOutput`, through a `MidiSendTap`) to that instrument's first node. The instrument plays it beside its own events and those of the MIDI tracks routed to it. A send that goes (removed, bypassed, switched off, playing elsewhere, its track removed) leaves its notes to end there: the instrument ends those the send still holds and no other source does (`InstrumentChannel::releaseNotesOf`), as for a MIDI track routed away.
- **The divider** (p. 1105): the inserts above it play what comes into the track live, from its MIDI input or `midi.inject`, after the input transformers and before the track records it, so the track records and monitors what they play (`MidiSequencer::processInput`). They don't play the track's parts, whose notes they played already when they were recorded; the inserts below the divider play both, as all inserts do without it. A MIDI track routed to an instrument track comes with its own effects; the instrument track's effects play its own events only, as with the modifiers. A MIDI track routed away from an instrument ends the notes its effects played there too: `MidiSequencer::holds` counts them with its own. They play while the transport stands too.
- **The effects** (`src/engine/processors/midieffects.h`) are built-in processors, `processors::MidiEffect`s: they handle events at their sample offsets and leave audio alone, take their parameters as parameter events or from the track's sequencer, and end the notes they started when the notes that made them end, so a stop or a jump, where the sequencer ends its notes, ends theirs too. When their output is full, note-offs take the places of other events, so no note hangs; the arpeggiator ends its note in the next block instead.
  - **Arpeggiator:** plays the held notes one after another, a step per note value of the tempo (`rate` 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 or 1/32), from the first note-on on: `mode` up, down, up and down (the top and bottom notes once each turn) or in the order played, over one to four `octaves`, each note as long as `gate` per cent of a step and as loud as the note it comes from. Up to 16 notes are held at a time. A step whose note-on finds no room in a full output is left out, and so are the steps whose time has passed by the next block: they don't play late.
  - **Chord generator:** plays a chord for every note, the note its root: major, minor, diminished, augmented, sus2, sus4, major 7, minor 7, 7, power chord or octave. Where two chords share a pitch it plays once and ends with the last of them; a note's note-off ends the chord its note-on started, also after the chord was changed.
  - **Transformer**, a transformer-style effect: the notes that meet its `condition` (all notes, or those whose pitch, MIDI 1.0 velocity or channel, 1 to 16, lies from `minimum` to `maximum`) are transposed by `value` (beyond 0 to 127 they are deleted), get it as their velocity or have it added (velocities stay from 1 to 127), move to channel `value` (1 to 16) or are deleted. A note-off and poly pressure go where their note-on went. Where two notes end up on one pitch and channel (two channels set to one, or a note transposed onto one that passes), it plays once and ends with the last of them, as in the chord generator. It is a rule of the rule editor's kind with a fixed shape and plays through the input transformers' `LogicalStage` ([midi-rules.md](midi-rules.md)).
- **In the inspector:** the MIDI Inserts and MIDI Sends sections show the slots with their effects, the divider, Pre and the sends' outputs, and each effect's control panel ([inspector.md](inspector.md)).
- **Automation:** a lane's target `midiEffect` with `number` (the slot: 0 to 3 the inserts, 4 to 7 the sends) and `parameter` automates a parameter of the effect in the slot ([automation.md](automation.md)); `parameter.list`, `parameter.get` and `parameter.set` have the effects' parameters too, and the automation tracks offer them. The track's strip plays the lanes as parameter events among the sequencer's events (`midi::g_effectEvents`), which the sequencer hands to the effect in the slot at their samples, so the effect changes there for any block size. The inserts above the divider play before the automation of the block arrives, so they get it a block late, at the block's start. A slot that gets another effect, or none, loses its lanes in the same undo step.
- **Real time and determinism:** the sequencer makes the effects on the command thread when the render snapshot changes, keeping the instance of an effect that stays in its slot so its state goes on, and runs them on the audio thread on buffers it allocated beforehand. An effect that stops playing (removed, replaced, switched off or bypassed) loses its state, and so does one that moves across the divider or to another output. When the effects that play change, the sequencer ends every note it sounds at the next block, those the effects played too, so none hangs; the notes held go on without sounding until they are played again. The arpeggiator counts its steps in frames from its first note and takes each step's length from the tempo where the step starts (the project's tempo map in the transport block, `TransportBlock::tempoMap`), so an arpeggio comes out the same for every block size (rule 6), also under tempo changes and ramps. The steps it leaves out after a full output take the tempo at the block's first frame, so under a ramp the steps after them can start at frames that depend on the block size; the output was incomplete then anyway.

## Not yet

Dragging the divider between the slots, as the reference does (the inspector chooses its place from a list); the effects through the plugin API (`BuiltinPlugin` leaves them out of `builtin:` plugins); the inserts above the divider taking their automation on time, all in #1144; sends to MIDI output ports, which MIDI tracks don't play to yet ([midi-devices.md](midi-devices.md)); more effects and an arpeggiator that follows the bars instead of its first note.
