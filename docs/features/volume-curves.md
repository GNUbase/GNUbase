# Event volume and volume curves

The volume of audio events and their volume curves (#139, reference manual pp. 398-402). Fades are in [fades.md](fades.md), how events play in [playback.md](playback.md).

## In the engine

- **Event volume**: an audio event's `volume` in dB, -144 to +6 (`audioEvent.set`), on everything the event plays.
- **Volume curve**: an audio event's `volumeCurve`, points by position, on top of its volume. A point has a `position` in the event's clip (sample frames), a `volume` in dB (-144 to +6) and a `curve` (-1 to 1) that bends the way to the next point: the gain goes from one point's to the next one's along `(1 - e^(-6cx)) / (1 - e^(-6c))`, the bend that automation curves have (`engine::automationBend`), straight for 0. Before the first point the curve is level with it, after the last one with that; points at the same position make a jump. Since positions are in clip frames, the curve moves and is copied with the event and stays with the audio when the event is resized or split; glue gives the event the loudest volume of the glued events and joins their curves where each played, lowered by their volumes' differences to it; conforming a clip to the project's sample rate scales the positions.
- **Playback** (`engine::curveGain` in `src/engine/audio/fades.h`): the render snapshot has each event's points as linear gains; the playlist puts them on the project's samples, and the audio thread multiplies every sample by the event volume, the curve and the fades. Where the curve is level, a block has one gain, as without a curve: no point inside it, and the same gain at its first and last frame (not at the frame after it, where a jump can return to the first frame's gain).
- **Commands**: `audioEvent.setVolumeCurve` (the whole curve, sorted, checked), `event.removeVolumeCurve` (Remove Volume Curve on events) and `range.volumeCurve` (the curves of the audio events in a range level at a volume inside it, unchanged outside it, with jumps at its edges; volume 0 is Remove Volume Curve on a range, p. 401). Each is one undo step, so MCP and Lua have them too.
- **DAWproject**: the curve is GNUbase-only, in `gnubase.json`; other applications play the events without it.

## In the event display

`ui::EventDisplay` (`src/ui/eventvolume.cpp`) shows and edits them through the engine API.

- **Volume lines**: `<fade-lines>` ([canvas.md](canvas.md)) draws an audio event's volume times its curve as a line in `stroke-color`, from silence at the bottom of the lane to +6 dB at its top (`ui::g_volumeLineTop`), so 0 dB is half way up, with the shape the engine plays (`engine::curveGain`). Events at 0 dB without a curve have none; the selected ones show squares at their points.
- **Waveforms** (pp. 399, 402): an audio event's waveform is drawn with its volume and curve, each pixel's peaks scaled by the gain at its middle (`ui::Waveform::setVolume`), also while the event volume control or a curve point is dragged. Events inside audio parts are drawn as their peaks are.
- **Event volume control** (p. 401): a square at the bottom left of a selected audio event, 16 dp in from its start, past the edge that resizes it; events less than 32 dp wide show none, as the reference shows it only once you zoom in. A press in the bottom 8 dp of a selected audio event, within 8 dp of it, drags it, after the fade handles (an unselected event shows no control, so a press there moves the event): down for less, up for more, as far as the line goes; the line follows the drag. On release every selected audio event gets the volume the pressed one got, in one `history.transaction` of `audioEvent.set`. Shift held while moving drags it a tenth as far, for finer steps, from where it is, so pressing or letting go of Shift doesn't make it jump (p. 402). While it moves, `.volume-readout` beside the control shows the pressed event's volume and, in brackets, its change since the press, such as `-6.0 dB (-6.0)`. The draw tool drags it too, as in the reference (p. 401), unless the press is on a curve point. The handle at the top of a selection range (p. 402) belongs to audio segments, which aren't here yet.
- **Mouse wheel** (pp. 382, 402): with Use Mouse Wheel for Event Volume and Fades on (`editing.wheelEventVolumeAndFades`, off by default, [preferences.md](preferences.md)), the wheel over an audio event changes its volume by 1 dB a line, up for louder, from -144 to +6 dB; with Shift it moves its fades ([fades.md](fades.md)). Each line is an `audioEvent.set` of the event under the mouse, an undo step of its own. Off, or over anything else, the wheel scrolls as before.
- **Draw tool** (p. 399): a click on an audio event adds a curve point there, a press on a point (or up to 6 dp from it) moves it by the drag's distance and a click without movement leaves it, both along the line's scale and inside the event, and no higher than the line's top; on release `audioEvent.setVolumeCurve` sends the curve, unless it is unchanged. With Ctrl held at the press, a drag draws a curve (p. 400): a point where the press was and one wherever the mouse has gone 4 dp sideways since the last, in place of the points between the first and the last of them, as one undo step. A point above the top (event volume plus point over +6 dB) is drawn at the top but moves from its own volume (#683): a drag down lowers it by the drag's distance, so it stays drawn at the top until it is below +6 dB in all, and a drag up leaves it as it is. The top stays +6 dB for every event, so that their lines compare, and a point goes no higher than +6 dB of its own (#829): below 0 dB of event volume the line can't reach the top, and a click or a drag above where the point can go puts it at +6 dB, drawn as high as it goes. A click on a point selects it, in place of the points selected before, and Shift and a click adds a point to them or takes it out again (p. 400); the volume line draws the selected points of a selected event in `stroke-color`, and Delete (`edit.delete`) removes them, as one `audioEvent.setVolumeCurve`, instead of the selected events. A point moved is no longer selected, and the selected points go when their event leaves the selection. The **erase tool** on a point of a selected event removes the point instead of the event; unselected events show no points, so there it removes the event. Points outside the event, which a split or resize leaves there, show no square, and neither tool takes them.
- **Remove Volume Curve** (`audio.removeVolumeCurve`, the Audio menu): `range.volumeCurve` on the selection range, else `event.removeVolumeCurve` on the selected audio events.

Not here yet: selecting points with a rectangle, nudging points, dragging a bend between two points, the volume curve editor's smart controls (p. 400), and audio segments (p. 402): the range selection tool's segment detection and its volume handle at the top of a range, which `range.volumeCurve` is for.
