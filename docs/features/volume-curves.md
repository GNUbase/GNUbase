# Event volume and volume curves

The volume of audio events and their volume curves (#139, reference manual pp. 398-402). Fades are in [fades.md](fades.md), how events play in [playback.md](playback.md).

## In the engine

- **Event volume**: an audio event's `volume` in dB, -144 to +6 (`audioEvent.set`), on everything the event plays.
- **Volume curve**: an audio event's `volumeCurve`, points by position, on top of its volume. A point has a `position` in the event's clip (sample frames), a `volume` in dB (-144 to +6) and a `curve` (-1 to 1) that bends the way to the next point: the gain goes from one point's to the next one's along `(1 - e^(-6cx)) / (1 - e^(-6c))`, the bend that automation curves have (`engine::automationBend`), straight for 0. Before the first point the curve is level with it, after the last one with that; points at the same position make a jump. Since positions are in clip frames, the curve moves and is copied with the event and stays with the audio when the event is resized or split; glue gives the event the loudest volume of the glued events and joins their curves where each played, lowered by their volumes' differences to it; conforming a clip to the project's sample rate scales the positions.
- **Playback** (`engine::curveGain` in `src/engine/audio/fades.h`): the render snapshot has each event's points as linear gains; the playlist puts them on the project's samples, and the audio thread multiplies every sample by the event volume, the curve and the fades. Where the curve is level, a block has one gain, as without a curve.
- **Commands**: `audioEvent.setVolumeCurve` (the whole curve, sorted, checked), `event.removeVolumeCurve` (Remove Volume Curve on events) and `range.volumeCurve` (the curves of the audio events in a range level at a volume inside it, unchanged outside it, with jumps at its edges; volume 0 is Remove Volume Curve on a range, p. 401). Each is one undo step, so MCP and Lua have them too.
- **DAWproject**: the curve is GNUbase-only, in `gnubase.json`; other applications play the events without it.

## In the event display

`ui::EventDisplay` (`src/ui/eventvolume.cpp`) shows and edits them through the engine API.

- **Volume lines**: `<fade-lines>` ([canvas.md](canvas.md)) draws an audio event's volume times its curve as a line in `stroke-color`, from silence at the bottom of the lane to +6 dB at its top (`ui::g_volumeLineTop`), so 0 dB is half way up, with the shape the engine plays (`engine::curveGain`). Events at 0 dB without a curve have none; the selected ones show squares at their points.
- **Event volume handle** (p. 401): a square at the top middle of a selected audio event, with the fade handles. A press in the top 8 dp of an audio event, within 8 dp of it, drags it, after the fade handles: down for less, up for more, as far as the line goes; the line follows the drag. On release every selected audio event gets the volume the pressed one got, in one `history.transaction` of `audioEvent.set`. The reference puts a fader at the event's bottom left; the handle at the top is the one its selection ranges have (p. 402), next to the fade handles.
- **Draw tool** (p. 399): a click on an audio event adds a curve point there, a press on a point (or up to 6 dp from it) moves it by the drag's distance and a click without movement leaves it, both along the line's scale and inside the event, and no higher than the line's top; on release `audioEvent.setVolumeCurve` sends the curve, unless it is unchanged. A point above the top (event volume plus point over +6 dB) is drawn at the top but moves from its own volume (#683): a drag down lowers it by the drag's distance, so it stays drawn at the top until it is below +6 dB in all, and a drag up leaves it as it is. The **erase tool** on a point removes it instead of the event.
- **Remove Volume Curve** (`audio.removeVolumeCurve`, the Audio menu): `range.volumeCurve` on the selection range, else `event.removeVolumeCurve` on the selected audio events.

Not here yet: the waveform drawn with the volume, drawing curves with Ctrl, selecting several points with a rectangle or Shift, nudging points, dragging a bend between two points, the volume curve editor's smart controls (p. 400), Shift for finer volume steps and the value shown while dragging, the mouse wheel's event volume, and audio segments (p. 402): the range selection tool's segment detection and its volume handle at the top of a range, which `range.volumeCurve` is for.
