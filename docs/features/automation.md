# Automation

Automation lanes, their points and how they play (#120).

- **Automation** (#120) lives in lanes (`AutomationLane`): one per track and target, with a read flag (write modes come with #122) and points. A target is the channel's `volume`, `pan` or the combined panner's `panLeft` and `panRight` ([mixer.md](mixer.md)), its `mute` button on tracks with audio channels (a MIDI track's mute would have to silence its notes as they play, so it isn't automated yet), a parameter of the track's built-in `instrument`, a parameter of the built-in `plugin` in one of the channel's slots (`slot` and `parameter`, as `parameter.list` names them), or a MIDI `controller` (number and channel), `pitchBend` or `channelPressure` on MIDI and instrument tracks. A point has a position in its track's time base, a value in the target's unit (dB for volume, -1 to 1 for the pans, 0 or 1 for mute, the parameter's range, 0 to 1 for MIDI) and the shape of the segment that starts at it: `step` holds the value, `linear` ramps to the next point, `curve` bends the ramp by `curve` (-1 to 1). As the reference manual describes (p. 870: ramp curves for continuous parameters, step curves for on/off ones), mute and the stepped parameters of instruments and plugins (`parameter.list`, where mute is a parameter too) have step curves: their points are always steps with whole values. A mute lane that plays overrides the mute button, which mutes again when the lane stops reading or has no points; solo works as before. A plugin's lanes go with it when it is removed or moves to another channel, and stay when it moves to another slot of its channel (the reference manual, p. 872), in the same undo step; a duplicated track's lanes automate the copies of its plugins. Plugins other than the built-in ones are automated once they report their parameters (#283). Like the track's events, points are in ticks on musical tracks, so automation follows the tempo map, and in samples on linear ones, so it keeps its time when the tempo changes; switching the time base or the sample rate keeps their time. Points at one position play in the order they were added: the last one is the value from there on, which makes jumps. With `automation.set` `followsEvents` (a setting, off by default, not part of the project or undo) moving an audio event or a MIDI part moves the points in its range with it and replaces the points where it lands, in the same undo step.

## Playback

- **Automation** plays in each channel's `ChannelStrip` (`src/engine/audio/automation.h`), which wraps the channel's processor (the sequencer, instrument channel or pass-through) and, on audio channels, a `ChannelFader` for volume, mute and the panner. The command thread publishes the channel's lanes to the strip's `AutomationPlayer`, which turns them into parameter events with sample offsets for every transport segment: a value at each point, a step exactly at its sample, and a ramp as one event whose `ramp` field says over how many frames the value moves in a straight line. The fader glides along it frame by frame, so a gain ramp has no zipper noise and is sample-accurate for any block size. The fader glides along curves as straight pieces between points of a 64-sample grid counted from the segment's first point. Mute fades out and in over 5 ms from the sample of its point on, so it doesn't click; the mute button fades over a block instead, since it changes the graph. Processors that don't glide get the value of each grid point at that point: instrument and plugin parameters as parameter events, MIDI controller lanes as controller, pitch-bend or channel-pressure events in the channel's event stream, only where the value changes at MIDI 1.0's resolution (7 bits, 14 for pitch bend). All of it renders the same for any block size, after cycle jumps and from any locate. A ramp's steps fill at most half of a block's events, so notes keep their room; the end of a ramp always arrives. On a channel with plugin slots the source node plays the automation and keeps the plugins' events, keyed by the slot's index, in the channel's state; each slot node's `SlotStrip` adds its slot's events to its plugin's events, since the source node always runs before the slots. After a start, a locate, a cycle jump or a change of the channel's processor or of a slot's plugin (e.g. a new instrument, or a plugin that has become active, whose graph may reach the audio thread after the automation) the player sends every lane's value at the new position at once; while stopped it sends the values at the cursor when the cursor or the automation changes. A lane that doesn't read leaves its target at the channel's static value. All of this allocates nothing on the audio thread.

## DAWproject

- In DAWproject, automation of volume, pan and MIDI controllers is written as track-level `Points`: volume as a linear gain, pan normalized, curves as eight linear pieces, steps as `hold`. The combined panner's, mute, the instruments' and the plugins' lanes stay in GNUbase's entry.
