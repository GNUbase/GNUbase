# Automation

Automation lanes, their points and how they play (#120). The project window shows and edits them on automation
tracks ([automation-tracks.md](automation-tracks.md)).

- **Automation** (#120) lives in lanes (`AutomationLane`): one per track and target, with a read flag (write modes come with #122) and points. A target is the channel's `volume`, `pan` or the combined panner's `panLeft` and `panRight` ([mixer.md](mixer.md)), its `mute` button on tracks with audio channels and on MIDI tracks, a parameter of the track's built-in `instrument`, a parameter of the built-in `plugin` in one of the channel's slots (`slot` and `parameter`, as `parameter.list` names them), a parameter of the channel `strip`'s sections on tracks with audio channels (`parameter`: the pre section, the equalizer, the gate, the compressor and the limiter, [channel-strip.md](channel-strip.md)), a parameter of the `midiEffect` in one of the MIDI insert or send slots of MIDI and instrument tracks (`number`, 0 to 3 the inserts and 4 to 7 the sends, and `parameter`, [midi-effects.md](midi-effects.md)), or a MIDI `controller` (number and channel), `pitchBend` or `channelPressure` on MIDI and instrument tracks. A point has a position in its track's time base, a value in the target's unit (dB for volume, -1 to 1 for the pans, 0 or 1 for mute, the parameter's range, 0 to 1 for MIDI) and the shape of the segment that starts at it: `step` holds the value, `linear` ramps to the next point, `curve` bends the ramp by `curve` (-1 to 1). As the reference manual describes (p. 870: ramp curves for continuous parameters, step curves for on/off ones), mute and the stepped parameters of instruments, plugins, MIDI effects and the channel strip (its switches, cut slopes and EQ band types; `parameter.list`, where mute is a parameter too) have step curves: their points are always steps with whole values. A mute lane that plays overrides the mute button, which mutes again when the lane stops reading or has no points; solo works as before. A file's points of mute and stepped-parameter lanes (from builds before #621, other applications or changed by hand) load as steps with whole values. A built-in plugin's lanes have the values set for its parameters as static values, and for parameters not set those of the plugin's state, so its state keeps the values it holds (the reference manual, p. 872: a parameter without automation keeps its value). A plugin's lanes go with it when it is removed or moves to another channel, and stay when it moves to another slot of its channel (the reference manual, p. 872), in the same undo step; a duplicated track's lanes automate the copies of its plugins. Plugins other than the built-in ones are automated once they report their parameters (#283). Like the track's events, points are in ticks on musical tracks, so automation follows the tempo map, and in samples on linear ones, so it keeps its time when the tempo changes; switching the time base or the sample rate keeps their time. Points at one position play in the order they were added: the last one is the value from there on, which makes jumps. Before the first point a lane has the first point's value and after the last point the last one's, unless the project uses virgin territory; a terminator point ends the curve up to the next point ([virgin-territory.md](virgin-territory.md), #124). With `automation.set` `followsEvents` (a setting, off by default, not part of the project or undo) moving an audio event or a MIDI part moves the points in its range with it and replaces the points where it lands, in the same undo step.

## Playback

- **Automation** plays in each channel's `ChannelStrip` (`src/engine/audio/automation.h`), which wraps the channel's processor (the sequencer, instrument channel or pass-through) and, on audio channels, a `ChannelFader` for volume, mute and the panner. The command thread publishes the channel's lanes to the strip's `AutomationPlayer`, which turns them into parameter events with sample offsets for every transport segment: a value at each point, a step exactly at its sample, and a ramp as one event whose `ramp` field says over how many frames the value moves in a straight line. The fader glides along it frame by frame, so a gain ramp has no zipper noise and is sample-accurate for any block size; so does the channel strip's pre gain, which plays as a linear gain like volume and whose events go with the fader's to the strip's sections (`StripSections`), which apply all their events at their samples ([channel-strip.md](channel-strip.md)). The fader glides along curves as straight pieces between points of a 64-sample grid counted from the segment's first point. Mute fades out and in over 5 ms from the sample of its point on, so it doesn't click. The mute button is the mute lane's static value, so it fades the same way, and a lane that starts or stops reading while the button is on switches between the two in one change, without letting audio through. On a MIDI track, which has no fader, the strip mutes the track's events from the sample of the mute's point: no notes, controllers or other events go out while it is muted, from its parts or its MIDI input. The notes that sound end there with note-offs, and then the pedals that hold notes and are down, at any value (sustain, CC 64; sostenuto, CC 66; hold 2, CC 69), go up, so no note hangs; in a full block both take the places of other events (`EventBuffer::addEnd`), whose values wait for unmuting as if the mute had dropped them. The strip knows each pedal's value from the events that pass it and from its track's own chase, which a routed MIDI track's sequencer gives its strip alone, while the instrument chases the track by time with the others ([playback.md](playback.md)). A mute lane or the button that unmutes lets new notes through and, at its sample, sends the latest value of each controller, program, pitch bend and channel pressure that the mute dropped, per MIDI channel and in the order of each kind's latest event, as a chase sends them by time (a bank select before the program change that came after it), and sets the pedals that the mute released back to their values, also a continuous pedal's half pedal, so the instrument ends up where the track's events left it (a pitch bend doesn't stay where it was), as a locate's chase would set it (the reference manual, p. 1610, chases when positioning and when soloing). They go out after the values that the automation sends again at that sample (`AutomationPlayer::play` gives the strip a copy of them): the channel's program, MIDI volume, pan and MIDI automation that a start, a locate, a jump, new automation, a new processor or being heard again sends, so the track's later values win over them, as where a mute lane unmutes, also for a kind whose chase setting is off. A MIDI automation lane's new value at that sample (a step, the end of a ramp, a ramp's step) is the latest of its kind, as the track's own events are: it goes out after what waits and replaces it. The strip finds those values among what its source passes on at their sample, also when the source leaves one out. What doesn't fit into the block next to the block's own events follows at the start of the next block, after the values sent again there and unless an event of its kind came in between. A soloed-out or disabled track sends none of it: the values wait until it is heard again. Heard again after playback moved while it was muted or silent, it leaves the kinds its channel's program, MIDI volume, pan and MIDI automation send to the chase that follows the channel's values, if the chase settings chase that kind, and the kinds of its track's own chase in that block, and forgets what the mute kept of them before it sends the rest, also a value that the track's MIDI input sent into the mute, as a jump does ([track-list.md](track-list.md)). A start, a locate or a cycle jump forgets them, found as the track's sequencer finds a jump: its chase sets what is in effect where playback lands. The strip keeps these values in `DroppedMidi`, fixed arrays in the channel's state, with a list through the kinds that wait in the order of their latest events, so that a kind dropped again moves to its end in constant time, however often kinds alternate. Channel ids start at 1 in every project, so a channel's state can reach the next project's track with that id: the first strip of another project (`ChannelStrip::playsProject`, `RenderSnapshot::opened`) forgets what the old track's mute kept and its pedals; the rest of the channel's MIDI state stays, so the notes the old track let through still end with their note-offs or a mute. It only knows its own track's events: if another track playing the same instrument changed one of these controllers meanwhile, unmuting sets the muted track's value, where a chase would take the later of the two. Direct monitoring can't follow automation, so a track with mute automation is monitored through the graph instead ([latency-and-monitoring.md](latency-and-monitoring.md)). Processors that don't glide get the value of each grid point at that point: instrument and plugin parameters as parameter events, MIDI effect parameters as parameter events that the track's sequencer hands to the effect in the slot at their sample (`midi::g_effectEvents`), MIDI controller lanes as controller, pitch-bend or channel-pressure events in the channel's event stream, only where the value changes at MIDI 1.0's resolution (7 bits, 14 for pitch bend). All of it renders the same for any block size, after cycle jumps and from any locate. A ramp's steps fill at most half of a block's events, so notes keep their room; the end of a ramp always arrives. A built-in plugin's static values reach it only through its lanes, not also through `PluginSlot::setParameters` (`SlotInstances` leaves them out), so a static value can't arrive a block before the automation that overrides it. On a channel with plugin slots the source node plays the automation and keeps the plugins' events, keyed by the slot's index, in the channel's state; each slot node's `SlotStrip` adds its slot's events to its plugin's events, since the source node always runs before the slots. After a start, a locate, a cycle jump or a change of the channel's processor or of a slot's plugin (e.g. a new instrument, or a plugin that has become active, whose graph may reach the audio thread after the automation) the player sends every lane's value at the new position at once; while stopped it sends the values at the cursor when the cursor or the automation changes. A lane that doesn't read leaves its target at the channel's static value. All of this allocates nothing on the audio thread.

## DAWproject

- In DAWproject, automation of volume, pan, mute and MIDI controllers is written as track-level `Points`: volume as a linear gain, pan normalized, curves as eight linear pieces, steps as `hold`, mute as `BoolPoint`s of the channel's `Mute` parameter. Reading `project.xml` takes them back, mute's points as steps (also `RealPoint`s, rounded). The combined panner's, the instruments', the plugins', the MIDI effects' and the channel strip's lanes stay in GNUbase's entry.

- `tests/automation_tests.cpp`: mute lanes as step curves that override the button; switching between the button and mute automation lets no audio through at block sizes 64 and 512, and the channel is heard afterwards; a MIDI track's mute ends notes and releases pedals in a full block, and a value whose place its end takes goes out when it unmutes; what unmuting sends comes in the order of the latest events, also of kinds that alternate more often than there are kinds, a bank select before a later program change, after the channel's values, also where the source leaves one out, a pedal at its value, and what doesn't fit follows in the next block, before a controller lane's new value there; a track heard again after a move forgets what its mute kept of the kinds chased for it, and another project's track on the channel gets nothing of the old one's; files' ramps on mute and stepped lanes load as steps; a built-in plugin's state values stay across play, locate and cycle jumps. `tests/plugin_api_tests.cpp`: a built-in plugin's state values and the values set are clamped, stepped ones rounded, and NaN, infinite ones and unknown ids skipped.
- `tests/sequencing_tests.cpp`: a MIDI track's mute lane ends its sounding notes at the point's sample and silences new ones, with and without the button; its mute releases the pedals that hold notes there; unmuting, by the lane or the button, sends the latest controllers, program and pitch bend that the mute dropped from the track's parts and its MIDI input, and a controller lane's new value where a mute lane unmutes after them, at block sizes 64, 100 and 333 and from a start inside the mute; a locate while the button mutes the track chases into the mute; a project loaded in place of another gets nothing of what the old project's MIDI track kept, nor its pedals.
- `tests/monitoring_tests.cpp`: a directly monitored track with mute automation that mutes is silent.
- `tests/dawproject_tests.cpp`: mute lanes round-trip through the project file and as `BoolPoint`s through `project.xml`; plugin lanes stay out of it and come back from GNUbase's entry.
