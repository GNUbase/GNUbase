# Automation

Automation lanes, their points and how they play (#120).

- **Automation** (#120) lives in lanes (`AutomationLane`): one per track and target, with a read flag (write modes come with #122) and points. A target is the channel's `volume`, `pan` or the combined panner's `panLeft` and `panRight` ([mixer.md](mixer.md)), a parameter of the track's built-in `instrument`, or a MIDI `controller` (number and channel), `pitchBend` or `channelPressure` on MIDI and instrument tracks. A point has a position in its track's time base, a value in the target's unit (dB for volume, -1 to 1 for the pans, the parameter's range, 0 to 1 for MIDI) and the shape of the segment that starts at it: `step` holds the value, `linear` ramps to the next point, `curve` bends the ramp by `curve` (-1 to 1). Like the track's events, points are in ticks on musical tracks, so automation follows the tempo map, and in samples on linear ones, so it keeps its time when the tempo changes; switching the time base or the sample rate keeps their time. Points at one position play in the order they were added: the last one is the value from there on, which makes jumps. With `automation.set` `followsEvents` (a setting, off by default, not part of the project or undo) moving an audio event or a MIDI part moves the points in its range with it and replaces the points where it lands, in the same undo step.

## Playback

- **Automation** plays in each channel's `ChannelStrip` (`src/engine/audio/automation.h`), which wraps the channel's processor (the sequencer, instrument channel or pass-through) and, on audio channels, a `ChannelFader` for volume and the panner. The command thread publishes the channel's lanes to the strip's `AutomationPlayer`, which turns them into parameter events with sample offsets for every transport segment: a value at each point, a step exactly at its sample, and a ramp as one event whose `ramp` field says over how many frames the value moves in a straight line. The fader glides along it frame by frame, so a gain ramp has no zipper noise and is sample-accurate for any block size. The fader glides along curves as straight pieces between points of a 64-sample grid counted from the segment's first point. Processors that don't glide get the value of each grid point at that point: instrument parameters as parameter events, MIDI controller lanes as controller, pitch-bend or channel-pressure events in the channel's event stream, only where the value changes at MIDI 1.0's resolution (7 bits, 14 for pitch bend). All of it renders the same for any block size, after cycle jumps and from any locate. A ramp's steps fill at most half of a block's events, so notes keep their room; the end of a ramp always arrives. After a start, a locate, a cycle jump or a change of the channel's processor (e.g. a new instrument, whose graph may reach the audio thread after the automation) the player sends every lane's value at the new position at once; while stopped it sends the values at the cursor when the cursor or the automation changes. A lane that doesn't read leaves its target at the channel's static value. All of this allocates nothing on the audio thread.

## DAWproject

- In DAWproject, automation of volume, pan and MIDI controllers is written as track-level `Points`: volume as a linear gain, pan normalized, curves as eight linear pieces, steps as `hold`. The combined panner's lanes stay in GNUbase's entry.
