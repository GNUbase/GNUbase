# Fades and crossfades

Fades, crossfades and auto fades of audio events and clips (#138). How they play is in [playback.md](playback.md).

- **Fades** (#138): audio events and clips have a fade in and a fade out, each a length in samples and a shape (`linear`, `exponential`, `logarithmic`, `sCurve`, `equalPower`; `audioEvent.setFades`, `clip.setFades`). An event's fades are part of the event and move with it; a clip's fades apply wherever the clip's start or end is played. A **crossfade** belongs to the later of two overlapping events on a track and names the earlier one (`crossfade.set`, `crossfade.remove`): its mode is `equalGain` (the two curves add up to 1), `equalPower` (their squares add up to 1) or `custom` (two shapes of their own), and the fade in and the fade out each have their own length, so crossfades can be asymmetric. A crossfade plays only while the later event starts inside the earlier one and ends after it, both on the same track and neither muted (`crossfadeApplies`); after an edit that ends this it rests, and both events play their own fades at those ends. **Auto fades** are project settings that a track can override (`autoFades.set`): short fades in and out, and crossfades wherever an event starts or ends on top of another one, 1 to 500 ms long. They don't stack with an event's own fades: an auto fade goes only where an event has no fade of its own at that end, and where an event with a fade of its own starts or ends on top of another one, its fade is the auto crossfade's and the event behind fades with the mirror image over the same stretch.

## DAWproject

- Fade lengths are written to `project.xml` as the clips' `fadeInTime` and `fadeOutTime` in seconds, a crossfade that plays as the fades of its two events (one that rests writes the events' own fades); shapes, crossfade modes, clip fades and auto fade settings live in `gnubase.json`. A project from another application, or one without a valid `gnubase.json`, gets linear fades from these attributes, in seconds or beats, each clip only its own; where an event starts inside the one before it and ends after it, and both have a fade at the ends that meet, the two fades become an equal gain crossfade.
