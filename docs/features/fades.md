# Fades and crossfades

Fades, crossfades and auto fades of audio events and clips (#138). How they play is in [playback.md](playback.md).

- **Fades** (#138): audio events and clips have a fade in and a fade out, each a length in samples and a shape (`linear`, `exponential`, `logarithmic`, `sCurve`, `equalPower`; `audioEvent.setFades`, `clip.setFades`). An event's fades are part of the event and move with it; a clip's fades apply wherever the clip's start or end is played. A **crossfade** belongs to the later of two overlapping events on a track and names the earlier one (`crossfade.set`, `crossfade.remove`): its mode is `equalGain` (the two curves add up to 1), `equalPower` (their squares add up to 1) or `custom` (two shapes of their own), and the fade in and the fade out each have their own length, so crossfades can be asymmetric. A crossfade plays only while the later event starts inside the earlier one and ends after it, both on the same track and neither muted (`crossfadeApplies`); after an edit that ends this it rests, and both events play their own fades at those ends. **Auto fades** are project settings that a track can override (`autoFades.set`): short fades in and out, and crossfades wherever an event starts or ends on top of another one, 1 to 500 ms long. They don't stack with an event's own fades: an auto fade goes only where an event has no fade of its own at that end, and where an event with a fade of its own starts or ends on top of another one, its fade is the auto crossfade's and the event behind fades with the mirror image over the same stretch.

## The Fades menu's commands

Reference manual pp. 384-385, 388, 395. Each is one undo step in the engine, so MCP and Lua have them too.

- `range.fades` with `fade` `in` (Fade In to Range Start): the audio events on the range's tracks fade in from their start to the range's start, or to its end where the range covers their start. `out` (Fade Out from Range End): fade out from the range's end, or from its start where the range covers their end. `adjust` (Adjust Fades to Range): the fades at the event's edges that the range covers, both when the range is inside the event. A fade that no longer fits next to the one set gets shorter.
- `event.crossfade` (Crossfade, key X): several events crossfade with each other wherever one starts inside the one before it on its track and ends after it; a single event with the events on either side of it. Crossfades there already stay. Crossfades only join events that overlap: the reference also lengthens events whose clips would overlap, which isn't here yet.
- `event.removeFades` (Remove Fade In, Remove Fade Out, Remove Fades): the fades at those ends and the crossfades there.

## In the event display

`ui::EventDisplay` (`src/ui/eventfades.cpp`) shows and edits fades of audio events through the engine API.

- **Fade lines.** `<fade-lines>` (`ui::FadeLines`, [canvas.md](canvas.md)) over the waveforms draws each fade as its curve from the bottom of the lane to the top, with the shape the engine plays (`engine::fadeGain`); a crossfade that plays draws its fade in and fade out instead of the fades at the ends it joins, over the overlap unless it has lengths of its own. Auto fades have no lines, as in the reference (p. 396). The themes give the lines `stroke-color` and the handles `color`.
- **Fade handles** (p. 383): squares at the top of a selected audio event where its fade in ends and its fade out starts. A press in the top 8 dp of an audio event, within 8 dp of a handle, drags it, before the edges' resizing; the lines follow the drag. On release every selected audio event gets the length the pressed one got, in its own clip's frames and as far as it fits, in one `history.transaction` of `audioEvent.setFades`.
- **The Audio menu's Fades submenu** (`resources/ui/project.rml`): the commands above on the selection range (`audio.fadeInToRange`, `audio.fadeOutFromRange`, `audio.adjustFadesToRange`) or the selected audio events (`audio.crossfade`, `audio.removeFadeIn`, `audio.removeFadeOut`, `audio.removeFades`), and Open Fade Editor(s) (`audio.fadeEditor`). Crossfade on events that have a crossfade opens the crossfade editor for it instead, and so does a double click in a crossfade's zone, where its events overlap.
- **Fade dialog** (`resources/ui/fade-editor.rml`, pp. 385-387): the fade in's and fade out's curves and lengths in milliseconds, from the first selected event; OK gives them to every selected event as one undo step, the lengths only with Apply Length.
- **Crossfade editor** (`resources/ui/crossfade.rml`, pp. 389-395), the simple one: the mode (equal gain, equal power, custom), the fade out's and fade in's curves and lengths in milliseconds up to the overlap, and Remove. Equal gain takes the fade in's curve and mirrors it, equal power has none, custom both (`crossfade.set`).
- **Auto Fades dialog** (`resources/ui/auto-fades.rml`, pp. 396-397): Project > Auto Fades Settings (`project.autoFades`) for the project, the track list's context menu (`track.autoFades`) for the first selected track, with Use Project Settings. Auto fade in, fade out and crossfades, the length, the curves and the crossfades' mode, sent with `autoFades.set`.

The dialogs are documents with Lua that reads what they edit when they show (the attributes the display or `app::AppUi` set: `events`, `from` and `to`, `track`) and send it with the engine's commands. Not here yet: curves drawn with points, fade presets, As Default and the default fade, Apply Standard Fade In and Out, Restore and audition in the dialogs, the crossfade editor's waveform display, nudging, splice points, zoom and chaining, the mouse wheel's fades, removing a crossfade by dragging it out of the event, and clip fades through offline processing (#141).

## DAWproject

- A warped event's fades fit into its `fadeRoom` from `project.get` ([warp.md](warp.md)), which the fade handles and
  the fade editor dialog keep them within, instead of its length.
- Fade lengths are written to `project.xml` as the clips' `fadeInTime` and `fadeOutTime` in seconds, a crossfade that plays as the fades of its two events (one that rests writes the events' own fades); shapes, crossfade modes, clip fades and auto fade settings live in `gnubase.json`. A project from another application, or one without a valid `gnubase.json`, gets linear fades from these attributes, in seconds or beats, each clip only its own; where an event starts inside the one before it and ends after it, and both have a fade at the ends that meet, the two fades become an equal gain crossfade.
