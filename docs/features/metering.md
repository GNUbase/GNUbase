# Metering and loudness

Level meters for every channel and the main mix, the master meter's true peak and the loudness meter after EBU R 128 (#111, reference manual pp. 533-540). This is the engine part; the meters in the mixer's channel strips (#107) and the right zone's meter display come with their UI tickets.

## How it works

- **Level meters** (`LevelMeter` in `src/engine/audio/meters.h`). Each channel strip measures its output after the fader, the panner and muting (`ChannelStrip::fade`), per audio channel of its output (both sides of a mono channel's panner on a stereo destination), in the channel's `ChannelState`, so the meter lives as long as the channel and survives new graphs. The audio thread publishes through atomics: every 100 ms the peak, the largest sample of those 100 ms; the RMS level of the last 300 ms, a window of ten slices whose sums of squares a ring keeps, at the end of every slice; and their maxima since the last reset. Nothing allocates or locks. MIDI channels have no audio channels and no meter.
- **The main mix** is measured as the device plays it, after auditioning, on the main mix bus's device outputs (`AudioEngine::meterMainMix`): a level meter, and the loudness meter, which also is the master meter's true peak meter. The master meter's RMS settings (reference manual p. 540) are user settings: `meter.rmsResolution`, how much of the signal its RMS level averages, 1 to 1000 ms (300 by default; the ring's ten slices share the window's frames, or fewer slices of one frame each for windows that short), and `meter.rmsAes17`, which adds AES17's 3 dB so that a sine's RMS level is its peak level. The channels' meters keep 300 ms without the offset.
- **The UI reads every meter once a frame** without a command and without JSON (`Engine::readMeters` with a `MeterFrame`: the channels to read go in, their levels, the main mix and the loudness come out, in vectors that keep their memory). The audio thread raises each channel's held peak after every block, with a compare-and-swap, and the reader takes it with an exchange, so a peak shorter than a frame is never missed and a display that reads per frame doesn't move in 100 ms steps; the true peak is held the same way. There is one such reader: the UI's windows (the right zone, the mixer windows) share what it reads, since a second reader would take the first one's peaks. A channel listed twice in a frame, shown by the inspector and the mixer at once, reads once and gets the same reading both times. A level meter's frame peak is never above its maximum: the audio thread publishes the maximum before it raises the held peak, and the reader takes the peaks before it reads the maxima, so the maximum is below a peak only when a reset (`meter.reset`, `meter.resetOnStart`) or a channel that came back cleared it, and the reader limits each peak to the maximum it read. A peak from before a reset is dropped that way; only a block under way when the command thread resets can publish its maximum once more, and the frame shows that until the next block. The audio thread's first block after a reset replaces the held peak with its own instead of raising it, so the limit only covers frames read before that block, and channels that block doesn't measure until they come back: raised, a quieter block would write nothing, and a reader that took the peak from before the reset could still read the reset's maximum of 0 instead of the block's, since nothing orders the audio thread's relaxed store of the maximum before its load of the held peak, and that block's peak would reach no frame. `meter.get`'s `peak`, the last 100 ms, can still reach back before a reset; and a frame's true peak has no such limit, since its maximum only counts while the transport plays. The channels' meters are found under a mutex that only the command thread, when it attaches the channel strips to a new graph, and the reader take; the audio thread never does.
- **Loudness** (`LoudnessMeter`, ITU-R BS.1770-4, EBU Tech 3341 and 3342). The channels are K-weighted (`analysis::kWeighting`) and summed, each weighted 1. Every 100 ms step the meter publishes the momentary loudness (the last 400 ms) and the short-term loudness (the last 3 s). While the transport plays it also measures, with blocks that start where playback started, so none holds audio from while the transport stood: the maxima of both, the integrated loudness of 400 ms blocks every 100 ms, gated at -70 LUFS and then 10 LU below the loudness of the blocks above that gate, and the loudness range of 3 s blocks every second, gated at -70 LUFS and 20 LU below their loudness, from the 10th to the 95th percentile. The blocks go into histograms of 0.1 LU from -70 LUFS up that keep each bin's energy and count, so the measurement runs for any length in fixed memory on the audio thread; the gates are exact to the bin, the range to 0.1 LU.
- **True peak**: each channel 4x oversampled through a 96-tap Kaiser-windowed sinc interpolator, centred on a tap so that it reads the samples and the points 1/4, 1/2 and 3/4 between them, flat within 0.01 dB up to 0.4 times the sample rate. The peak of the last 100 ms, and its maximum while playing.
- **A device reset** (`audio.reset`, a dropout restart or a driver's request) keeps the loudness measurement when the sample rate stays the same, as the performance measurement does: momentary and short-term loudness and the true peak start again with the signal, and the blocks with playback, so none spans the restart. Another sample rate, a fallback device that plays while the device is lost, or a device that `audio.setDevice` opens starts it over.
- **Channels that come back**, such as a mono channel's second side when it moves to a stereo destination again (`pannerOutput`, the one place that says how wide a panner makes a channel), start without what they measured before.
- **Commands.** `meter.get` reads the channels (all, or those given), `main` and `loudness`, in dBFS, dBTP and LUFS; null for silence and for values not measured yet. `meter.reset` resets the maxima and starts the loudness measurement over and announces `meter.reset`. The user setting `meter.resetOnStart` (the reference's Reset on Start) starts the loudness meter and the main mix's maxima over whenever playback starts.

## Tests

`tests/meter_tests.cpp`: a sine's peak and RMS level, the maxima and their reset; the RMS resolution, its range and AES17, also at 44.1 and 96 kHz; the per-frame reading, which holds a single sample's peak past the 100 ms window, loses no peak to a block that ends while it reads, nor that of a quieter block right after a reset, stays within its maximum while the audio thread runs and resets come from another thread, and holds nothing from before a reset, also with `meter.resetOnStart`; EBU Tech 3341's steady sines (cases 1 and 2, also at 44.1 and 96 kHz), gating (cases 3 to 5) and changing levels (the short-term loudness of case 9 and the momentary loudness of case 12, after every block) within 0.1 LU; true peaks of sines at a quarter, a sixth and an eighth of 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, between the samples, which the test also checks stay below them, at -6 and +3 dBTP within +0.2 and -0.4 dB; EBU Tech 3342's loudness range cases 1 to 3 within 1 LU; the integrated loudness of noise against the offline `analysis::integratedLoudness`; the true peak of a sine at a quarter of the sample rate that falls between the samples; measuring only while playing and resetting; silence; the loudness measurement through a restart at the same and at another sample rate, and when a lost device returns, with and without a fallback device; and `meter.get`, `meter.reset`, `meter.resetOnStart`, `meter.rmsResolution`, `meter.rmsAes17`, `audio.reset` and `Engine::readMeters` through the dummy device, and `Engine::readMeters` on a thread of its own while the device runs and the graph is rebuilt, which the TSan job checks. `tests/rtcheck_tests.cpp` runs both meters at every RMS resolution, two sample rates and changing channel counts under the RT checker.
