# Metering and loudness

Level meters for every channel and the main mix, the master meter's true peak and the loudness meter after EBU R 128 (#111, reference manual pp. 533-540). The right zone's Meter tab shows the master meter and the loudness meter; the meters in the mixer's channel strips (#636) come with their UI ticket.

## How it works

- **Level meters** (`LevelMeter` in `src/engine/audio/meters.h`). Each channel strip measures its output after the fader, the panner and muting (`ChannelStrip::fade`), per audio channel of its output (both sides of a mono channel's panner on a stereo destination), in the channel's `ChannelState`, so the meter lives as long as the channel and survives new graphs. The audio thread publishes through atomics: every 100 ms the peak, the largest sample of those 100 ms; the RMS level of the last 300 ms, a window of ten slices whose sums of squares a ring keeps, at the end of every slice; and their maxima since the last reset. Nothing allocates or locks. MIDI channels have no audio channels and no meter.
- **The main mix** is measured as the device plays it, after auditioning and, while channels listen, with the listen dim and the listen bus ([solo-mute-listen.md](solo-mute-listen.md)), on the main mix bus's device outputs (`AudioEngine::meterMainMix`): a level meter, and the loudness meter, which also is the master meter's true peak meter. The master meter's RMS settings (reference manual p. 540) are user settings: `meter.rmsResolution`, how much of the signal its RMS level averages, 1 to 1000 ms (300 by default; the ring's ten slices share the window's frames, or fewer slices of one frame each for windows that short), and `meter.rmsAes17`, which adds AES17's 3 dB so that a sine's RMS level is its peak level. The channels' meters keep 300 ms without the offset.
- **The UI reads every meter once a frame** without a command and without JSON (`Engine::readMeters` with a `MeterFrame`: the channels to read go in, their levels, the main mix and the loudness come out, in vectors that keep their memory). The audio thread raises each channel's held peak after every block, with a compare-and-swap, and the reader takes it with an exchange, so a peak shorter than a frame is never missed and a display that reads per frame doesn't move in 100 ms steps; the true peak is held the same way. There is one such reader: the UI's windows (the right zone, the mixer windows) share what it reads, since a second reader would take the first one's peaks. A channel listed twice in a frame, shown by the inspector and the mixer at once, reads once and gets the same reading both times. A level meter's frame peak is never above its maximum: the audio thread publishes the maximum before it raises the held peak, and the reader takes the peaks before it reads the maxima, so the maximum is below a peak only when a reset (`meter.reset`, `meter.resetOnStart`) or a channel that came back cleared it, and the reader limits each peak to the maximum it read. A peak from before a reset is dropped that way; only a block under way when the command thread resets can publish its maximum once more, and the frame shows that until the next block. The audio thread's first block after a reset replaces the held peak with its own instead of raising it, so the limit only covers frames read before that block, and channels that block doesn't measure until they come back: raised, a quieter block would write nothing, and a reader that took the peak from before the reset could still read the reset's maximum of 0 instead of the block's, since nothing orders the audio thread's relaxed store of the maximum before its load of the held peak, and that block's peak would reach no frame. `meter.get`'s `peak`, the last 100 ms, can still reach back before a reset. A frame's true peak is held and limited the same way by `truePeakHold`, which the audio thread publishes before it raises the held true peak, and whose reset replaces it in the next block. The channels' meters are found under a mutex that only the command thread, when it attaches the channel strips to a new graph, and the reader take; the audio thread never does.
- **Loudness** (`LoudnessMeter`, ITU-R BS.1770-4, EBU Tech 3341 and 3342). The channels are K-weighted (`analysis::kWeighting`) and summed, each weighted 1. Every 100 ms step the meter publishes the momentary loudness (the last 400 ms) and the short-term loudness (the last 3 s). While the transport plays it also measures, from start to stop (reference manual p. 538): its blocks start where playback started, and the K-weighting filters start again there, so none holds audio from while the transport stood; where playback stops within a 100 ms step, what played of that step counts too, with the 400 ms and 3 s blocks that end at the stop (the part of their oldest step that they reach taken at that step's mean loudness): the 400 ms block counts towards the momentary maximum and the integrated loudness, the 3 s block only towards the short-term maximum, since the loudness range keeps its 3 s blocks every second from the start. A reset while the transport plays starts a fresh step the same way. It measures the maxima of both, the integrated loudness of 400 ms blocks every 100 ms, gated at -70 LUFS and then 10 LU below the loudness of the blocks above that gate, and the loudness range of 3 s blocks every second, gated at -70 LUFS and 20 LU below their loudness, from the 10th to the 95th percentile, and the time it measured, to the sample. The blocks go into histograms of 0.1 LU from -70 LUFS up that keep each bin's energy and count, so the measurement runs for any length in fixed memory on the audio thread; the gates are exact to the bin, the range to 0.1 LU.
- **True peak**: each channel 4x oversampled through a 96-tap Kaiser-windowed sinc interpolator, centred on a tap so that it reads the samples and the points 1/4, 1/2 and 3/4 between them, flat within 0.01 dB up to 0.4 times the sample rate. The peak of the last 100 ms, its maximum while playing (the loudness meter's True Peak), and its maximum since the master meter's last reset, also while the transport stands (`truePeakHold`, the master meter's peak marks and Peak Max.). Right after playback starts, the points between the samples count once the interpolator holds only samples from after the start, from the 24th sample on, so the points between the first 12 samples or so (0.25 ms at 48 kHz) aren't measured; the samples themselves are.
- **A device reset** (`audio.reset`, a dropout restart or a driver's request) keeps the loudness measurement when the sample rate stays the same, as the performance measurement does: momentary and short-term loudness and the true peak start again with the signal, and the blocks with playback, so none spans the restart. Another sample rate, a fallback device that plays while the device is lost, or a device that `audio.setDevice` opens starts it over.
- **Channels that come back**, such as a mono channel's second side when it moves to a stereo destination again (`pannerOutput`, the one place that says how wide a panner makes a channel), start without what they measured before. The audio thread clears their values before it publishes the new channel count, which it stores with release and readers load with acquire, so a reader that sees the channel come back doesn't read its old values, also on ARM64.
- **Commands.** `meter.get` reads the channels (all, or those given; a channel the project doesn't have, also one deleted while no device runs, is refused), `main` and `loudness`, in dBFS, dBTP and LUFS; null for silence and for values not measured yet. `meter.reset` resets the maxima and starts the loudness measurement over and announces `meter.reset`; with `meters` it resets only the main mix's maxima, its true peak hold included (`main`, the master meter's reset) or only the loudness meter's values, its true peak maximum included (`loudness`, Reset Loudness), and leaves the channels' maxima alone. The user setting `meter.resetOnStart` (the reference's Reset on Start) starts the loudness meter and the main mix's maxima over whenever playback starts.

## The meter display

The Meter tab of the project window's right zone (`ui::MeterPanel`, reference manual pp. 533-538) reads the meters once a frame with `Engine::readMeters`, as the UI's one reader of them, through `EngineBinding::watch`, so the window draws only when what it shows changed. Its Master and Loudness buttons at the bottom switch between two pages (the state class `.loudness` on `.meter-display`, the selected button `.checked`), and each page has its own reset, which sends `meter.reset` with its `meters` (pp. 535 and 538):

- **Master**: a true peak meter (p. 533), a bar per channel of the main mix (`<meter>`, -60 to +6 dB) with its true peak since the last frame, its RMS level as the bar's `rms` (the fill again over the dimmed true peak, p. 535) and its true peak maximum (`LoudnessReading::truePeakHold`, `truePeakHold` in `meter.get`'s `main`, which counts as `peakMax` does, also while the transport stands, and only the master meter's reset clears) as the bar's peak mark; below them RMS Max. and Peak Max., the largest of the channels' maxima, Peak Max. the true peak's (p. 535), and Reset RMS Max. and Peak Max.
- **Loudness**: two bars on the EBU +9 scale (-41 to -14 LUFS), the momentary loudness with the integrated loudness as its mark and the short-term loudness with its maximum as its mark, then Momentary Max., Short-Term (its maximum), Integrated, Range, True Peak (the largest channel's maximum) and Time (minutes and seconds measured), and Reset Loudness. A loudness value shows `--` until there is one and for silence, as `LoudnessReading` has none then; only the levels and the true peak show `-inf` for silence.
