# Linux MIDI

Linux MIDI ports come from two backends (#42): the ALSA sequencer (`src/platform/midi/alsaseq.cpp`, backend `alsa`) and JACK MIDI (`src/platform/midi/jackmidi.cpp`, backend `jack`). `midi::makeBackends()` returns them in that order, before the dummy backend. Like every MIDI backend they are reached through `midi.ports`, `midi.setPort`, `midi.refresh` and `midi.monitor`; the port model and the other backends are in [architecture.md](../architecture.md#midi-ports).

## ALSA sequencer

- **Ports.** Every exported port of the other sequencer clients: sound cards' ports, the kernel's Midi Through port, applications' ports, and PipeWire's MIDI bridge, which shows PipeWire's MIDI nodes as sequencer ports. Ports others can read from are inputs, ports others can write to outputs; a port that is both is listed twice under one id. The system client and ports marked as not exported are left out.
- **Ids.** A port is listed by its sequencer name ("Midi Through Port-0"). Its id is "client name:port name" ("Midi Through:Midi Through Port-0"), numbered from the second port with the same names ("USB MIDI:Port 1 #2"): client numbers change when devices come and go, names don't.
- **One client.** The backend is a sequencer client "GNUbase", opened with the first listing or port, so an engine that never uses MIDI leaves the sequencer alone. An open port is a port of that client ("GNUbase in", "GNUbase out"), subscribed to the port it was opened for; closing deletes it, which ends the subscription. A thread of the client's own reads what arrives and calls the receivers.
- **Messages.** The sequencer's events become MIDI 1.0 bytes and back through alsa-lib's MIDI event coder; SysEx isn't carried yet.
- **Time.** Input is stamped with the host time it arrives at. Outputs send messages due now at once and leave later ones to a sequencer queue of the client's, scheduled relative to the queue's current time. The queue's timer decides how exactly they leave: to a millisecond with the high-resolution timer that most distributions' kernels use for the sequencer.
- **Hot-plug.** The client follows the sequencer's announcements: other clients and ports starting, changing or exiting are reported as changed ports.
- **No sequencer.** Without `/dev/snd/seq` (the `snd-seq` module isn't loaded, as in WSL and most containers), listing fails and `midi.ports` leaves the backend's ports out, with a warning in the log.

## JACK MIDI

- **Ports.** The MIDI ports of a running JACK server, jackd2's or PipeWire's JACK layer: other clients' MIDI outputs are inputs and their MIDI inputs outputs. A port's id and name are its full JACK name ("system:midi_capture_1"). Under PipeWire these are PipeWire's MIDI nodes, which the ALSA backend lists too.
- **Only with the JACK audio backend.** JACK MIDI is on only while one of GNUbase's JACK audio devices is open (opened, not necessarily started): the JACK audio device counts itself in `g_jackAudioDevicesOpen` (`src/platform/jacklibrary.h`). Where libjack is PipeWire's, a server always answers and its MIDI ports are PipeWire's MIDI nodes, which the ALSA backend lists already; an engine on PipeWire or ALSA audio would otherwise open every MIDI device twice and keep a client in the graph. When the first JACK audio device opens or the last one closes, the backend reports changed ports, so that `midi.refresh` opens or closes its ports. Someone who picks the JACK audio backend under PipeWire still sees those devices through both backends.
- **When JACK runs.** The backend loads libjack when it is first used, through the same loader as the JACK audio backend, and connects as a client "gnubase-midi" of its own, next to the audio device's "gnubase". JACK is optional: without libjack, a running server or an open JACK audio device the backend simply has no ports and connects nothing. When the server goes away or the last JACK audio device closes the backend reports changed ports. The listing of the `midi.refresh` that follows drops the client if no JACK audio device is open by then, and the same refresh closes its open ports, so the client leaves the graph; the next listing connects again. A device that only reopens (`audio.setDevice`, `audio.reset`, and the resets the JACK device asks for itself) is open again by the time that refresh runs, so the client and its open ports stay.
- **Ports of its own.** An open port is a port of the client (`in_n`, `out_n`), connected to the port it was opened for; closing unregisters it. The client is active from the start, so its process callback runs every cycle.
- **Real time.** The process callback reads each open input's events and calls its receiver, and writes each output's due messages; it neither allocates nor locks. Receivers are therefore called on JACK's real-time thread, which the receiver contract (`port.h`) allows; the engine's receiver pushes into the lock-free input queue. Opening and closing ports hand slots to the process thread through atomic flags, and closing waits until the thread is out of the port. Outputs carry what is sent to the process thread through a lock-free queue per port.
- **Time.** An event in a cycle's input arrived during the cycle before: its frame is mapped onto the host times of the two cycles' starts. Outputs place a message at its frame in the cycle it is due in; messages due before the cycle go to its start, later ones wait, together with those sent after them.
- **The order of clients.** The MIDI client and the audio device's client run in one cycle, in an order JACK chooses. When the audio client runs first, a cycle's input reaches the audio thread one block later, at the start of its block.
- **Hot-plug.** Ports registered or unregistered on the server are reported as changed ports. JACK's callbacks may come on the process thread (JACK 1 has no other), so they only take notes, which a thread of the backend reports every 20 ms.

## Tests

In `tests/mididevice_tests.cpp`, against the port contract that every MIDI backend keeps:

- **ALSA:** a sequencer client of the test's own with a port that passes everything on to its subscribers is the loopback; the test checks that it is noticed coming and going and that its id follows the naming above, and also loops through Midi Through when `snd-seq-dummy` is loaded. Skipped without `/dev/snd/seq`. The `linux-x64-gcc` CI job installs the runner kernel's extra modules, loads `snd-seq-dummy` and sets `GNUBASE_REQUIRE_ALSA_SEQ=1`, so that the test fails rather than skips; where the modules can't be loaded, the step warns and the test skips.
- **JACK:** a JACK client of the test's own passes its MIDI input to its MIDI output each cycle; with a JACK audio device open, the test checks that its ports are noticed going away and coming back, and loops through it. Another test checks that the backend has no ports and opens none while no JACK audio device is open, and that the ports appear and go away, with a change reported each time, as a JACK audio device opens and closes, and that with ports open, closing and reopening the device keeps them receiving and lists none of GNUbase's own ports. Skipped without a JACK server; the `linux-x64-gcc` CI job starts jackd (`tools/start-jack.sh`) and sets `GNUBASE_REQUIRE_JACK=1`. Without libjack the backend has no ports and refuses to open any.
- The hidden `[hardware]` contract test covers both backends on a machine's own ports, e.g. `GNUBASE_TEST_MIDI_LOOPBACK="Midi Through Port-0"`.
