# Mixer

The channels' faders and panners (#269); the mixer windows are in [mixer-window.md](mixer-window.md), the channel
strip's sections before the fader in [channel-strip.md](channel-strip.md).

- **Fader.** Every audio channel has a volume in dB, from -144 (silence, -∞ dB) to the project's maximum volume, default 0 dB. `channel.set` stops values beyond the ends there instead of refusing them. The maximum is a project setting, `maxVolume` in `project.set`: +6 dB (the default) or +12 dB; lowering it brings faders and the points of volume automation lanes above it down, in the same undo step, so that the project file, which checks them against it, still opens with GNUbase's own data (#961). MIDI channels have no audio, so no fader or panner: they send MIDI volume and pan instead (below).
- **Panner by routing.** Which panner a channel gets depends on its format and its destination's (`pannerFor` in `src/engine/document/channelformat.h`), worked out when the project compiles:
  - A mono channel on a stereo destination has a position, `pan` from -1 (left) to 1 (right), placed with the project's stereo pan law. Its channel node outputs stereo: the graph gives it a second buffer (`NodeDesc::output`) that the panner writes.
  - A stereo channel on a stereo or a mono destination has the **balance panner** (`panner` `balance`, the default): `pan` turns down the side panned away from, the center leaves both at 0 dB, as the built-in `gainPan` does. On a mono destination its balance decides how much of each side goes into the mono mix. The **combined panner** (`panner` `combined`, stereo channels only) places the left and the right channel each on its own with the pan law, at `panLeft` and `panRight` (default -1 and 1, which leaves the channel as it is). Both at the same position sum the channel to mono there, which is louder, as in the reference. Linking the two positions while they move is the UI's job (#107): the engine keeps both.
  - **Pan bypass** (`panBypass`): a mono channel plays at the center, a stereo one hard left and right.
  - Destinations wider than stereo have no panner until the surround panner (#188): the format conversion places mono in C (or in L and R at the pan law's center) and stereo in L and R ([channel-formats.md](channel-formats.md)). Wider channels have no panner either.
- **Pan law** (`panLaw` in `project.set`): how much a mono signal panned to the center is turned down on each side, so it doesn't sound louder there than panned to one side. The side panned toward is at 0 dB with every law (`panGains`):
  - `0dB` doesn't turn the center down: the side panned away from goes down in a straight line, like the balance panner.
  - `equalPower` keeps the power of both sides together: the sine of (1 - pan) π/4 on the left and of (1 + pan) π/4 on the right, -3.01 dB at the center.
  - `-3dB` (the default), `-4.5dB` and `-6dB` raise those to the power that turns the center down by exactly that much.
  The pan law also sets where the format conversion puts mono on destinations without C.
- **Parameters and smoothing.** Volume, pan, panLeft and panRight are the fader's parameters (`ChannelFader` in `src/engine/audio/automation.h`), automatable like volume and pan ([automation.md](automation.md)). When `channel.set` gives one of them a new value, the fader glides there in a straight line over 5 ms (`g_faderSmoothing`) instead of jumping, while playing and while stopped; a new lane, a locate or a cycle jump still takes the value at once. The panner mode, bypass and pan law are fixed for a graph, which a change rebuilds.
- **MIDI volume and pan.** A MIDI channel has `midiVolume` and `midiPan`, 0 to 127 (64 is the center pan), or none (`null`, the default), which leaves the instrument's own. The channel sends them to its instrument as MIDI volume (CC 7) and pan (CC 10) on its MIDI channel, as static lanes of its automation (`channelAutomation` in `src/engine/audio/automation.h`): whenever they change (each by itself, so that a part's own CC 7 and CC 10 stay; with the program when that changes), and again when playback starts, locates or jumps, so the instrument has them wherever playback begins. A silent MIDI track (muted, soloed out or disabled) sends neither, and once it is heard again only what changed meanwhile ([track-list.md](track-list.md)). Automation of controller 7 or 10 on that MIDI channel overrides them while it plays.
- **MIDI channel.** A MIDI channel's `midiChannel` (`channel.set`, 0 to 15, or `null` for Any, the default) is the MIDI channel its program, bank, MIDI volume and pan go out on (the Output Group and Channel of the reference manual, p. 349); Any sends them on MIDI channel 1. Changing it sends them all again on the new channel, whose instrument doesn't have them yet. The render snapshot carries it as `RenderChannel::midiChannel`, the chase leaves those controllers to the channel's own on it (`channelChaseKinds`), `project.get` shows it, the project file keeps it in GNUbase's entry, track presets take it along with the patch, and undo takes it back. The inspector's Routing section has it as Channel (`select[control=midiChannel]`: Any, 1 to 16) on MIDI tracks.
- **Events on the track's MIDI channel** (p. 351). A MIDI track set to a channel plays everything on it instead of the events' own channels: its parts' notes, note expressions and controller events (SysEx has no channel), its controller, pitch bend and channel pressure automation (the render snapshot gives the lanes' targets that channel, so automation of controller 7 or 10 overrides its MIDI volume and pan, and `automationLane.targets`, which offers them on channel 1, needs no other targets), and what its MIDI input plays, notes, controllers, pitch bend, pressure and program changes (`MidiSequence::inputChannel`; a key's note-off ends the note its note-on played, even if the channel changed in between). A drum sound's or a sound slot's own channel still wins over the track's, as on Any. Any keeps every event's channel, with the automation on the channel its lane names. The events themselves keep their channels, so a track set back to Any plays them as they were recorded. Two automation lanes of one controller on different channels both play on the track's channel, where the last one wins.
- **Mute, solo and listen** silence channels as the track list's buttons do ([track-list.md](track-list.md)), with exclusive solo, solo defeat and listen ([solo-mute-listen.md](solo-mute-listen.md)).
- **DAWproject** stores the volume as a linear gain in `Volume` (its `max` the project's maximum volume) and `pan` normalized in `Pan` (0.5 is the center). The panner mode, its positions, pan bypass, MIDI volume and pan, the pan law and the maximum volume go into GNUbase's extra entry; a project read from `project.xml` alone gets the defaults, with volumes above +6 dB brought down to it.
