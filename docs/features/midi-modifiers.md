# MIDI modifiers

Settings of a MIDI or instrument track that change how its notes play, not the notes themselves (#134, reference manual pp. 1096, 1099–1102, 1107). They are the channel's `modifiers` in `channel.set` and `project.get`, so also the MCP tool `channel_set` and `gnubase.channel.set` in Lua, and they are saved in GNUbase's entry of the project file.

- **The settings** (`midi::Modifiers` in `src/engine/midi/modifiers.h`): `transpose` in semitones (-127 to 127), `velocityShift` added to MIDI 1.0 velocities (-127 to 127), `velocityCompression` and `lengthCompression` as `[numerator, denominator]` (each 1 to 16), two random generators `random` and two ranges `range`, and `bypass`, which plays the notes as they are. `channel.set` changes only what it is given, also within each generator and range (`random: [{}, {"target": "pitch"}]` changes the second one's target).
- **In what order:** a note is transposed and gets its random pitch; a note that ends up beyond 0 to 127 doesn't play. Its velocity is multiplied by the compression (rounded), then the shift and its random velocity are added, kept from 1 to 127; values above 1/1 with a negative shift expand the range of velocities, as on p. 1100. Then the ranges, first and second: `velocityLimit` moves velocities to the nearer end of the range, `velocityFilter` leaves out notes with velocities outside it, `noteLimit` moves pitches by octaves into it (a range narrower than an octave can leave a note on the far side), `noteFilter` leaves out notes with pitches outside it. Lengths are multiplied by the length compression, then get their random length, at least one tick; random positions move the start. While no velocity setting is on, velocities keep their 16 bits; otherwise the modifiers work in MIDI 1.0 velocities.
- **Random generators** (p. 1101) have a target, `off`, `position`, `pitch`, `velocity` or `length`, and a minimum and maximum, the minimum not above the maximum: positions and lengths in ticks up to a quarter note either way, pitches and velocities up to 127. Each note gets a value from the minimum to the maximum, chosen by a hash of its part's and its own id, so it plays the same way every time and rendering stays deterministic; a new target keeps the values that fit it. The inspector, switching a generator between a target in ticks (position, length) and one that isn't, keeps the numbers it shows instead, within the new target's limits, as ticks of 480 to a quarter note: Pitch -12 to 12 becomes Position -12 to 12. Two generators on one target add up.
- **The parts' notes:** the render snapshot (`compilePart` in `src/engine/document/rendersnapshot.cpp`) applies the modifiers to the notes of the track's parts, so the sequencer plays and chases the notes as modified, and the notes in the document, the editors and `project.get` stay as they are. A part plays only what lies in it: a note moved or lengthened is still cut at its part's end, one moved to or past the end doesn't play, and one moved before its part's start (or the project's) starts there, with its length. The manual doesn't say where such notes go; this keeps the part's start as its edge, as its end is. Poly pressure presses the key its note plays (`midi::PressedNotes`: of the part's notes of its pitch and channel, the one that sounds at its position, the latest started if several do) and goes nowhere if that note doesn't play, as live input's does; on a MIDI track with a MIDI channel of its own, which plays both on it, a note of the pitch on any channel. Poly pressure without such a note moves by the transposition (the track's, the part's and global), not through the drum map, the chord track or a sound slot, and doesn't play beyond 0 to 127. The MIDI sends before the modifiers get the pressure where the notes they get play.
- **A part's transpose and velocity** (p. 1107): `midiPart.set` `transpose` (semitones) and `velocity` (added to MIDI 1.0 velocities), each -127 to 127, add to the track's transpose and velocity shift for the part's notes, also while the track's modifiers are bypassed; a part that follows global transpose plays its transpose folded into that sum while Keep Transpose in Octave Range is on ([transpose.md](transpose.md), #896). `project.get` and `midiPart.changed` show them with the part. Parts glued together (`event.glue`) keep the first part's values; the notes of the others get the difference of what the two parts play them with written into them, so that they play as before — for the transpose the difference at each note's position, which the octave-range fold can make other than the parts' transpose difference. A note the difference would transpose beyond 0 to 127 can't, so the glue is refused. Velocities stay from 1 to 127: a note the difference pushes beyond that is stored at the end and plays that much softer or louder (a glue isn't refused for it, since a loud note in a quieter part is common). With a velocity compression other than 1/1 (and the modifiers not bypassed) the difference goes in divided by it, as the track compresses a stored velocity before it adds the part's: the notes keep their sound for the compression set at the glue, as near as its steps allow, while the velocity that needs stays from 1 to 127 (at 1/2 a note of 120 in a part 30 louder plays at 90, which a stored 180 would give; stored at 127, it plays at 64). Poly pressure moves with the note it presses, else by the difference at its own position; poly pressure the difference would move beyond 0 to 127 refuses the glue too. The glued notes get new ids, so random modifiers, play probability and velocity variance choose their values anew.
- **Live notes** (p. 1099): the track's sequencer applies them to the notes played into it (`midi.inject`, MIDI input with MIDI thru), with a random value per note for pitch and velocity; positions and lengths don't apply. It remembers where each note-on went, so its note-off and poly pressure go to the same note, also after the modifiers changed, and a note left out has its note-off left out too. A second note-on for a key that is still held goes where the modifiers send it this time; if that isn't where the first one went, the first one's note ends first, so that the key's one note-off leaves no note hanging; if the block has no room for that note-off, it goes out at the start of the next block. The MIDI recorder records what was played, before the modifiers. An instrument track's modifiers apply to its own notes; those of the MIDI tracks routed to it come with their own track's modifiers.
- **In the app** (pp. 1099–1102, 1107): the inspector's MIDI Modifiers section shows a MIDI or instrument track's modifiers and changes them, and the info line shows the selected MIDI parts' Transpose and Velocity ([inspector.md](inspector.md)).
