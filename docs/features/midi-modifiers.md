# MIDI modifiers

Settings of a MIDI or instrument track that change how its notes play, not the notes themselves (#134, reference manual pp. 1096, 1099–1102, 1107). They are the channel's `modifiers` in `channel.set` and `project.get`, so also the MCP tool `channel_set` and `gnubase.channel.set` in Lua, and they are saved in GNUbase's entry of the project file.

- **The settings** (`midi::Modifiers` in `src/engine/midi/modifiers.h`): `transpose` in semitones (-127 to 127), `velocityShift` added to MIDI 1.0 velocities (-127 to 127), `velocityCompression` and `lengthCompression` as `[numerator, denominator]` (each 1 to 16), two random generators `random` and two ranges `range`, and `bypass`, which plays the notes as they are. `channel.set` changes only what it is given, also within each generator and range (`random: [{}, {"target": "pitch"}]` changes the second one's target).
- **In what order:** a note is transposed and gets its random pitch; a note that ends up beyond 0 to 127 doesn't play. Its velocity is multiplied by the compression (rounded), then the shift and its random velocity are added, kept from 1 to 127; values above 1/1 with a negative shift expand the range of velocities, as on p. 1100. Then the ranges, first and second: `velocityLimit` moves velocities to the nearer end of the range, `velocityFilter` leaves out notes with velocities outside it, `noteLimit` moves pitches by octaves into it (a range narrower than an octave can leave a note on the far side), `noteFilter` leaves out notes with pitches outside it. Lengths are multiplied by the length compression, then get their random length, at least one tick; random positions move the start. While no velocity setting is on, velocities keep their 16 bits; otherwise the modifiers work in MIDI 1.0 velocities.
- **Random generators** (p. 1101) have a target, `off`, `position`, `pitch`, `velocity` or `length`, and a minimum and maximum, the minimum not above the maximum: positions and lengths in ticks up to a quarter note either way, pitches and velocities up to 127. Each note gets a value from the minimum to the maximum, chosen by a hash of its part's and its own id, so it plays the same way every time and rendering stays deterministic; a new target keeps the values that fit it. Two generators on one target add up.
- **The parts' notes:** the render snapshot (`compilePart` in `src/engine/document/rendersnapshot.cpp`) applies the modifiers to the notes of the track's parts, so the sequencer plays and chases the notes as modified, and the notes in the document, the editors and `project.get` stay as they are. A part plays only what lies in it: a note moved or lengthened is still cut at its part's end, one moved to or past the end doesn't play, and one moved before its part's start (or the project's) starts there, with its length. The manual doesn't say where such notes go; this keeps the part's start as its edge, as its end is.
- **A part's transpose and velocity** (p. 1107): `midiPart.set` `transpose` (semitones) and `velocity` (added to MIDI 1.0 velocities), each -127 to 127, add to the track's transpose and velocity shift for the part's notes, also while the track's modifiers are bypassed. `project.get` and `midiPart.changed` show them with the part. Parts glued together (`event.glue`) keep the first part's values; the notes of the others get the difference of their part's values to those written into them, so that they play as before. A note the difference would transpose beyond 0 to 127 can't, so the glue is refused; velocities stay from 1 to 127, and with a velocity compression other than 1/1 the track compresses the difference too.
- **Live notes** (p. 1099): the track's sequencer applies them to the notes played into it (`midi.inject`, MIDI input with MIDI thru), with a random value per note for pitch and velocity; positions and lengths don't apply. It remembers where each note-on went, so its note-off and poly pressure go to the same note, also after the modifiers changed, and a note left out has its note-off left out too. A second note-on for a key that is still held goes where the modifiers send it this time; if that isn't where the first one went, the first one's note ends first, so that the key's one note-off leaves no note hanging.
- **In the app** (pp. 1099–1102, 1107): the inspector's MIDI Modifiers section shows a MIDI or instrument track's modifiers and changes them, and the info line shows the selected MIDI parts' Transpose and Velocity ([inspector.md](inspector.md)). The MIDI recorder records what was played, before the modifiers. An instrument track's modifiers apply to its own notes; those of the MIDI tracks routed to it come with their own track's modifiers.

## Not yet

Hermode tuning (pp. 1101–1103), Freeze MIDI Modifiers (pp. 1103–1104), the track input transformer (pp. 1097–1099), note names (C-2 to G8) in the ranges' fields for pitches (p. 1102), and link groups passing the modifiers on. Bounce MIDI taking the parts' transpose and velocity into account (p. 1123), Dissolve Part keeping them, and Merge MIDI in Loop applying the modifiers (pp. 1096, 1120) come with the MIDI functions' follow-ups (#838), which change the same functions.

## Checks

`tests/midimodifiers_tests.cpp`: each setting by itself through `midi::modifyNote` and `midi::modifyLength` (transpose, velocity shift and compression, length compression, random values within their limits and the same for a seed, each range mode, the ranges' order and bypass); `channel.set` and `project.get` with undo, what it refuses, and saving and loading, with a part's transpose and velocity; a part's transpose and velocity added to the track's, also bypassed, with undo; the notes of a part in the render snapshot for each setting, random positions and lengths that stay the same when the snapshot is compiled again, and notes injected live, with a note-off after the transpose changed and a note a range leaves out; a second note-on for a held key, to the same note and to another one; a note a random position moves before its part's start; glued parts with different transposes and velocities, and a glue refused. `tests/inspector_tests.cpp` drives the inspector's section and the info line's fields ([inspector.md](inspector.md)).
